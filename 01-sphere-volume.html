Section 7 · Problem 1

Volume from a measured radius.

PROPAGATION OF ERROR I
r = (6.20 ± 0.05) cm
V = (4/3)πr³

A sphere is measured with calipers. The single measurement carries an absolute uncertainty of ± 0.05 cm. Because volume scales as the cube of radius, the fractional uncertainty is amplified by a factor of three — a small mistake in the ruler becomes a much larger mistake in the volume.

Step 01 — Measurement

Read the instrument.

r = 6.20 cm
Δr = ± 0.05 cm
δr/r = 0.05 / 6.20 = 0.00806 (0.81 %)
Step 02 — Central value

Plug into the formula.

V = (4/3) π r³
V = (4/3) π (6.20)³
V = (4/3) π (238.328)
V = 998.31 cm³
Step 03 — Propagate

For a power law, fractions multiply.

V ∝ r³ ⇒ ΔV/V = 3·(Δr/r)
ΔV/V = 3 × 0.00806 = 0.0242 (2.42 %)
ΔV = 0.0242 × 998.31 = 24.16 cm³
Reported value
V = 998 ± 24 cm³
NOTE A 0.8 % error in r becomes a 2.4 % error in V. The cube exponent triples the relative uncertainty.
