PROBLEM

Find V(t)

An AC current I(t) = 2·sin(120πt) flows through a single resistor of R = 50 Ω. What's the voltage V(t) across the resistor?

METHOD · OHM'S LAW

For a pure resistor, Ohm's law holds at every instant:

V(t) = I(t) · R

No integration, no derivatives — voltage tracks current proportionally and instantaneously. There's no phase shift.

SUBSTITUTE
V(t) = [2·sin(120πt)] · 50 = 100·sin(120πt) [V]

The voltage is a sine wave with the same frequency, same phase, but amplitude scaled by R = 50.

PARAMETERS
Angular freq: ω = 120π rad/s Frequency: f = ω/(2π) = 60 Hz Period: T = 1/f ≈ 16.67 ms Current peak: I₀ = 2 A Voltage peak: V₀ = I₀·R = 100 V
RMS VALUES

For continuous AC delivery, what matters is the RMS (root-mean-square):

I_rms = I₀/√2 ≈ 1.41 A V_rms = V₀/√2 ≈ 70.7 V P_avg = V_rms·I_rms ≈ 100 W
KEY OBSERVATION

On the scope, the two traces rise and fall together — they cross zero at the same instants and peak at the same instants. That zero phase difference is the signature of a pure resistor. Add an inductor or capacitor and they'd separate.
