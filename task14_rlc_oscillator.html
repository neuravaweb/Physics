SAME EQUATION

Two Worlds, One Math

An RLC circuit and a mass on a spring with a dashpot obey identical second-order ODEs — only the symbols differ. Both decay sinusoidally when underdamped.

ELECTRICAL · RLC
L · d²Q/dt² + R · dQ/dt + Q/C = V(t) Inertia of current ← L Energy dissipation ← R Restoring force ← 1/C State variable ← Q (charge)
MECHANICAL · MASS-SPRING
m · d²x/dt² + b · dx/dt + k · x = F(t) Inertia of motion ← m Friction / damping ← b Spring stiffness ← k State variable ← x (position)

UNDERDAMPED SOLUTION

For both systems with no driving:

x(t) = A · e^(−γt) · cos(ω_d·t + φ) where γ = damping rate (R/2L or b/2m) ω₀ = natural freq (√(1/LC) or √(k/m)) ω_d = √(ω₀² − γ²) damped freq

Slide γ: small → ringing for ages, large → quick decay, very large → no oscillation at all (overdamped).

WHY IT MATTERS

Ideas like Q-factor, resonance, and bandwidth carry over directly. Tune your shock absorbers and you've tuned an RC filter. The math doesn't care.
