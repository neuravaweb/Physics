The Problem

The radius of a sphere is measured to be r = (6.20 ± 0.05) cm. Calculate the volume of the sphere and its associated uncertainty.

1

Calculate the Nominal Volume (V)

First, calculate the volume using the measured radius, ignoring the uncertainty for a moment. The formula for the volume of a sphere is:

V =
4/3
π r³

V =
4/3
π (6.20)³

V = 998.31 cm³
2

Determine Error Propagation Formula

To find the absolute uncertainty (ΔV), we take the derivative of the volume formula with respect to the radius (r). This represents how much the volume changes for a small change in radius.

ΔV ≈ |
dV/dr
| Δr

Derivative of (4/3)πr³ is 4πr²

ΔV = 4 π r² Δr
3

Calculate the Uncertainty (ΔV)

Substitute the measured radius (r) and the absolute uncertainty of the radius (Δr) into the derived formula.

ΔV = 4 π (6.20)² (0.05)

ΔV = 24.15 cm³

Final Reported Value

Following significant figure conventions, uncertainties are typically rounded to one significant digit (or two, depending on strict convention). The main value is then rounded to match the decimal place of the uncertainty.

V = (998 ± 24) cm³
