Section 7 · Problem 10

You can measure c with a chocolate bar.

LIGHT SPEED · KITCHEN EDITION
f = 2.45 GHz
ctrue = 2.998 × 108 m/s

A microwave oven traps a standing electromagnetic wave inside its metal cavity. The field has zero amplitude at the nodes and maximum amplitude at the antinodes. With the turntable disabled, the chocolate cooks fastest at the antinodes — leaving melted spots spaced by exactly half a wavelength.

Step 01 · The trap

Reflections build a standing wave.

The magnetron pumps EM waves at 2.45 GHz into a metal box. Waves bounce off the walls and superpose, producing a stationary pattern with fixed nodes (zero field) and antinodes (peak field).

E(x, t) = E₀ · sin(kx) · cos(ωt)
Step 02 · Hot spots appear

Antinodes cook the fastest.

Heating rate is proportional to ⟨E²⟩, which peaks at the antinodes. Disable the turntable, leave a chocolate bar in for ~40 seconds, and a row of melted spots emerges.

Step 03 · Measure the spacing

Spot-to-spot distance = λ ⁄ 2.

Consecutive antinodes are separated by exactly half a wavelength. A ruler reads d ≈ 6.0 cm between the centres of adjacent melted spots.

λ = 2 · d = 2 · 6.0 cm = 12.0 cm = 0.120 m
Step 04 · Compute the speed

c = f · λ.

The frequency is printed on the back of every microwave oven — typically 2.45 GHz. Multiply by the measured wavelength.

c = (2.45 × 10⁹ Hz) · (0.120 m)
c = 2.94 × 10⁸ m/s
Step 05 · Compare to truth

Percentage error.

|cmeas − ctrue| / ctrue
= |2.94 − 2.998| / 2.998
≈ 2.0 %
