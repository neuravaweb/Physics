PROBLEM

Find I at t = 3 s

The charge passing through a wire is given by:

Q(t) = 5t² + 5 [C]

What is the instantaneous current at t = 3 s?

DEFINITION

Current is the rate at which charge passes a point — the time derivative of charge:

I(t) = dQ/dt

Geometrically: the slope of the Q(t) curve, drawn as a dashed red tangent on the upper plot.

DIFFERENTIATE
Q(t) = 5t² + 5 d/dt [5t²] = 10t d/dt [5] = 0 I(t) = 10t [A]

The constant +5 carries no current — it's just an offset. Only the time-varying part contributes.

EVALUATE AT t = 3
I(3) = 10·(3) = 30 A

Use the slider — at t = 3 s the tangent on Q(t) has slope 30, and the lower plot reads 30 A. They have to match; that's what the derivative means.

SANITY CHECK

At t = 0: I = 0. The Q(t) curve is flat there (parabola vertex at (0, 5)) — zero slope, zero current. As t grows, Q curves upward more steeply, so the current rises linearly. ✓
